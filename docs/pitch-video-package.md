## POSITIONING BRIEF

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOOK SENTENCE:
  "We ran 50 years of human psychology experiments on AI.
   The results were not what I expected."

CORE PROMISE:
  Before: You deploy AI agents to simulate human behavior, but
          you're working on intuition — there's no controlled data
          showing whether AI actually reasons and cooperates the
          way humans do.
  After:  You have empirical answers. Same experiments, same
          payoff matrices, same round structure humans were tested
          with for decades — now run on AI, every reasoning trace
          captured, charted against published human baselines.

TARGET VIEWER:
  An AI researcher or builder who uses or studies LLM personas
  and has privately wondered: "Do these things actually behave
  strategically, or are they just pattern-matching the right words?"

ONE THING TO REMEMBER:
  AI cooperates more than humans at first — then defects at the
  end, exactly like we do.

PROOF ANCHOR:
  In Prisoner's Dilemma, human subjects cooperate 62% in round 1
  (Dal Bó & Fréchette 2011, AER, n=358, same payoff matrix).
  AI personas in our lab: 76%. But by round 4, both collapse to
  the same defection spiral.

CATEGORY CLAIM:
  The first behavioral lab running classic game-theory experiments
  on AI — with published human baselines overlaid.
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

## PRODUCTION CHECKLIST

### Before Recording

- [ ] Script open in teleprompter — use teleprompter.online (free, runs in browser). Position it just below your webcam on a second screen or tablet so your eyeline stays natural.
- [ ] Product open and pre-loaded at `game.atypica.ai` — have a **completed** Prisoner's Dilemma session already waiting at the game view, with reasoning traces visible in rounds 1–4. Do not run a live game during recording.
- [ ] Dashboard / home view loaded showing the cooperation rate chart for Prisoner's Dilemma.
- [ ] Browser cleaned: bookmarks bar hidden (`Cmd+Shift+B`), zoom set to 120%, no personal tabs visible.
- [ ] **Lighting**: place a window or softbox lamp in *front* of you (facing your face), not behind you. Your face must be brighter than the background.
- [ ] **Microphone**: external USB mic or AirPods Pro preferred over built-in laptop mic. Test for echo — record 10 seconds of silence and listen back.
- [ ] Phone + desktop notifications off (Do Not Disturb on both).
- [ ] Screen recorder ready: QuickTime (Mac, free) → File → New Screen Recording. Records screen + mic simultaneously.

### What to Record, in Order

- [ ] **3 takes of the hook only** (0:00–0:10) — on camera. Pick the most energetic delivery, not the cleanest. The pause before "not what I expected" should feel genuine.
- [ ] **Problem + bridge** (0:10–0:43) — on camera, one continuous take. Record 2 takes.
- [ ] **Screen recording: demo** — narrate live over the pre-loaded game session. Record the full walkthrough twice as a safety copy. Zoom into reasoning text as you read it aloud.
- [ ] **Chart reveal** — record an isolated close-up zoom into the cooperation chart (round 1 bars, then pan to round 4). This gives you a clean insert shot for editing.
- [ ] **Proof + CTA** (2:00–2:25) — on camera. 2 clean takes. End the CTA with confidence — do not trail off after the URL.

### Editing Notes

- Cut any clip that starts with "So..." or "Um..." — begin every cut on a hard consonant.
- Add captions before any other edit. Use CapCut (free, mobile or desktop) or Descript.
- **Sound design**: add one single audio hit — a low "thud" or deep "whoosh" — at the exact moment the round 4 defection decision appears. This is the emotional peak of the video. That one sound effect lifts perceived production quality more than any other edit.
- Do not use background music under the demo section — silence makes the reasoning text feel more deliberate and readable.
- Export: MP4, 1080p minimum. 16:9 for LinkedIn/YouTube. Crop to 9:16 for X/Twitter/Shorts.
